# World Bible (draft, 2026-10-14)

**Status:** Opus draft for the owner. Not canon until the owner confirms it; then it updates 07A, doc 14 and the `world-look-decision` memory. Sources: the two Deep Research reports in the StickGame ChatGPT project (art-style worlds; a deep analysis of the stick-figure animation series the owner loves), the owner's 2026-10-14 answers, and AGENTS.md.

**Thesis:** one movement game travels through many media. The **verbs never change** (Move, Jump, Rope). Each world changes only its **nouns**: what an anchor is, what is lethal, what moves, and how it looks and sounds. Keep the input vocabulary tiny and the world vocabulary enormous.

## Constitution (every world, no exceptions)

1. **Three verbs.** No new button or verb, ever: no attack, grab, cut, paint, hack or dash. Combat is swing impacts plus stomps (doc 05 §7).
2. **The rope is sacred.** Length = distance to the anchor centre on the lock tick; release keeps the swing's velocity. Worlds may move anchors, never retract, extend or stretch the rope. No elastic anchors.
3. **One highlighted anchor** (Targeting v2). Every anchor skin keeps one global token: a readable centre and halo, the clearest mark after the hero.
4. **Red is lethal, and lethal is red,** always with a non-colour cue as well (hatch pattern, bubbling edge, strobing arc, scanline break). No decorative saturated red in any palette.
5. **The rope is drawn dashed** in every world; its skin changes (pencil, thread, paint stroke, neon cable, signal line), its physics never do.
6. **One headline rule per section:** level A teaches it, B combines it with a known rope principle, C is the set piece that foreshadows the boss. The finale remixes; it never adds a fourth rule.
7. **Gameplay layer under a style adapter.** Collision is authored in level data; a pencil slash and a scan beam are the same `LethalHazard`. A world is a skin, a hazard kit and an anchor kit (GMR-120 plumbing).
8. **Qualities, never copies.** From the animation series: minimal silhouettes, causal choreography (anticipation, impact as a state change, momentum continuity), the camera as explanation, escalation that changes kind before magnitude. Never its characters, colour-coded cast, animator-vs-animation premise, desktop/window/cursor world, UI-as-weapon beats, choreography or sound.
9. **No real desk objects** in the drawn worlds (owner 2026-10-08): no rulers, compasses, paper clips, erasers or pens as props. Everything is drawn, cut, painted, lit or computed.
10. **Never a release-timing cue** (owner). A phased anchor may pre-pulse before it _appears or vanishes_: that tells you where the anchor will be, not when to let go. (Opus ruling; flag to owner.)

## The journey (proposed order, Opus pick)

The drawing comes alive and is copied from medium to medium, each copy more vivid, chased by **the Correction**, a living red stroke that tries to erase it (red = lethal, explained). Each chapter's medium escalates; each finale is the Correction in that medium.

| #   | Chapter        | World                        | Feel                         | Correction's form (boss) |
| --- | -------------- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| 1   | **The Mark**   | Pencil and ink sketchbook    | intimate, moody, hand-made   | The Red Scribble         |
| 2   | **The Cut**    | Cut and torn paper diorama   | tactile, theatrical, layered | The Cut Line             |
| 3   | **The Wash**   | Gouache and watercolour      | lush, flowing, dreamlike     | The Flood Stroke         |
| 4   | **The Signal** | Neon light city at night     | fast, rhythmic, electric     | The Night Press          |
| 5   | **The Upload** | Inside the machine (digital) | luminous, abstract, kinetic  | The Overwrite            |

Why this order: difficulty and spectacle rise together (quiet paper → neon speed → digital abstraction); each transition is a playable "copy" moment (page torn loose and cut out; the model painted over; the painting printed as a glowing poster; the poster scanned into the machine); and the digital finale, the owner's favourite energy, is the climax.

## Chapter 1: The Mark (pencil and ink)

**Look:** confident thin black pen over soft graphite, construction lines, deep tonal shadow on a cool blue-grey page (not flat beige), light shafts with graphite dust, a colossal half-sketched landmark far behind. Concept: [`0018-worlds/pencil.jpg`](../../assets/concepts/0018-worlds/pencil.jpg), **Opus pick for chapter 1**. Keep its atmosphere, depth and graphite light; when 1-1 is rebuilt, break the box silhouettes it inherited from our layout (organic tops, overhangs, root-hung undersides, like its top-left cliff).
**Music:** the synth core with felt-piano plucks and pencil-tap percussion; sparse.

| Section               | Headline rule                        | Signature hazard                                    | Anchor twist                                                        | Set piece                                                                           |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Margin Wilds       | swing basics, rope length = distance | red scribble pools and inked thorns                 | static ink rings                                                    | the camera pulls back: the whole page is one huge drawing being finished around you |
| 2. Construction Lines | anchors on drawn guide arcs          | red construction strokes that sweep along their arc | anchors travel along visible pencil arcs (path drawn before motion) | a diagram on the page rotates into a working drawn machine                          |
| 3. Inkstorm           | the page changes under you           | red ink spreading along the paper grain             | anchors ride the buckling, curling page                             | rain curls the page into a downhill escape                                          |

**Finale, The Red Scribble:** a living red stroke crosses out routes and narrows the page. Three weak points (knots in the stroke) take swing impacts; each hit tears part of the page loose and changes the arena; the last tears the page free and the hero is cut out with it.

**Music:** the full "cyberpunk future whimsy" palette: synth bass, arps, processed lead accents. This chapter is where music may time the world (with visual pre-cues always).

## Chapter 5: The Upload (inside the machine)

**Look:** the scanned drawing becomes light: crisp glowing data blocks and circuit-trace ledges with black outlines, signal-node anchors, light-stream rivers and pixel fragments far behind, electric blue, mint and white, a crystalline data-core tower. **Never** desktop windows, title bars, folders, cursors or app UI (do-not-copy list). Concept: [`0018-worlds/digital-b.jpg`](../../assets/concepts/0018-worlds/digital-b.jpg). (`digital.jpg`, with window panels and a cursor monolith, is rejected.)
**Music:** tracker arpeggios, granular textures, chopped motifs from chapters 1–4.

| Section           | Headline rule       | Signature hazard                                     | Anchor twist                                                | Set piece                                                            |
| ----------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Scan Fields    | the world assembles | a red scan bar that rasterises everything it crosses | anchors appear pixel by pixel ahead of the scan (pre-pulse) | the drawing is scanned in; you outrun the scan as the world resolves |
| 2. Memory Lattice | anchors on rails    | red overwrite blocks that fill cells                 | signal nodes riding branching bus rails                     | fall through a lattice that opens into an endless glowing grid       |

**Finale, The Overwrite:** the Correction's final form remixes every chapter's rule while red corruption consumes the system; its core nodes take swing impacts; the last swing goes out through the final open node, and the ending pulls back to the hero copied across every medium.

## Bosses (all chapters)

- **Health is the arena:** three hits, three arena states (no health bar). Each swing impact changes the geometry; failed plans create the next problem.
- **Weak points take swing impacts and stomps only,** and show their vulnerable face clearly. Speed and angle matter (GMR-119).
- **Hit → rebound → next rope,** never a cutscene reset. Roughly 30% boss behaviour, 70% boss-and-arena relationship.
- Phase changes alter the situation (a trusted anchor moves, the arena tilts), not just speed.

## Ruled out by canon (from the research)

A slower game-speed assist is an accessibility option that slows real time only, never the 60 Hz sim; runs are tagged.
