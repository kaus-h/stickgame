# 0009: world-art direction sheet (GMR-105)

Tool: ChatGPT image generation, GPT-6 at High effort, new chat in the StickGame project, 2026-10-08. Result: `assets/concepts/0009-world-directions.png` (1672×941). No reference images were uploaded, and no artist was named.

Prompt (verbatim):

> Create one 16:9 image: a 2x2 grid of four alternative world-art directions for the SAME gameplay moment from our notebook platformer, labeled A, B, C, D in small handwriting in each panel corner. Use only our own established look from this project (pen on college-ruled notebook paper, punched holes on the left edge). The moment, identical in every panel: our hero (the approved stick-figure design from this project's hero pose sheet: round slightly lumpy head drawn in one wobbly line, two hollow eye holes, short cross-stitched mouth, single-line limbs, tiny round hands, small solid black boots, NO scarf, no accessories) swings right on a dashed-line rope from a round anchor ring, between a low ceiling slab with small downward spikes and a row of short spiked floor posts, above a pool of red ink; a narrow landing ledge waits on the right. Gameplay rules that must hold in every panel: red is used only for deadly things (the ink pool and spike tips), the anchor ring is the single clearest mark, platforms read as solid, the background stays faint and low-contrast. The four directions: A "desk objects": platforms and ceiling are drawn stationery (stacked books, a ruler, an eraser block, a pencil) in pen hatching. B "taped paper": platforms are torn paper scraps stuck on the page with translucent tape, soft drop shadows. C "ink wash": heavy crosshatching, ink bleed and wet-pen texture, dramatic but readable. D "margin doodles": ballpoint scribbles, a sticky note as the ledge, doodled stars and arrows faintly in the margin. No text other than the panel letters. Do not imitate any specific living artist.

Notes for the owner pick:

| Panel | Direction      | Readability notes                                                                                                                                              |
| ----- | -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A     | Desk objects   | Clearest solids, and red appears only on hazards. The heavy hatching may compete with the hero at phone size.                                                  |
| B     | Taped paper    | The softest look, and the hero and anchor pop the most. Scraps have irregular edges, so collision must be drawn to rectangle edges (art aligned to collision). |
| C     | Ink wash       | The most atmospheric, but the dark masses crowd the play space and the background is not low-contrast. It is the riskiest for readability.                     |
| D     | Margin doodles | The most playful. The blue scribble fill is busy, and smiley faces in platforms could be misread as enemies.                                                   |

Recommendation: **A, with B's paper scraps for the background layer.** Stationery platforms give the clearest collision silhouettes and a strong notebook-world identity. A hybrid can be tested in the GMR-56 readability pass.
